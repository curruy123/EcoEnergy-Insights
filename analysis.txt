STATISTICAL TESTING:


Our first hypothesis was that there is a statistical difference in the index of total agriculture TFP production in countries from 2010 and 2021. 
Our null hypothesis was that there is not a significant difference in agriculture TFP production between countries in 2010 and 2021, 
with our alternate hypothesis being that there is a significant difference. In order to test this hypothesis, we used 
a paired t-test. A paired t test is the best in this case because we have a data group that has been measured twice 
(2010 and 2021). We found that the paired t p-value was 0.00376... which was indeed lower than 0.05 - the threshold in 
which the null is rejected. Thus, we could conclude that there was a significant statistical change in the TFP in 
countries from 2010 and 2011. Similarly, we also did this for our climate change analysis, which was measured through
C02 emissions for our third hypothesis. For this test, we compared the CO2 growth from 2018 and 2021. The p-value in 
this case was 0.00994324... which is also less than 0.05, making this a statistically significant result. There is a 
statistically significant change in cumulative CO2 growth for countries from 2018 and 2021. 


For our second hypothesis, we used a two sample t test instead. This t test is used for when there are two independent
 groups. In this case, we had compared the change in energy per capita between the US and China from the time period 
 1993 to 2021. We did these two countries as they have the largest CO2 emissions in the world. We found that the two 
 sample p values in this case was 6.979371745985823e-41, which was also statistically significant.
